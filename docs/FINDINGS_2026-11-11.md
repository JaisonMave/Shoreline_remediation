## 2. CRMS sites against oiled shoreline, and the 2010 event study

`scripts/match_crms_scat.py` -> `data/external/scat_dwh/crms_scat_match.csv`

| Group (ASSUMED rule) | Sites |
| --- | --- |
| Oiled: Light or worse oiling within 1 km | 20 |
| Unoiled: surveyed shoreline within 1 km, no oil within 5 km | 25 |
| Excluded | 296 |

Distance check, done first as asked. Only 4 of the 20 oiled sites lie within 50 m of the surveyed marsh
edge (median 203 m), and only one (CRMS0176) lies within 50 m of an oiled zone. Deepwater Horizon oil sat in
roughly the first 10 to 20 m of marsh. So the CRMS stations mostly sit beyond the oiled band.

The groups are also unbalanced: 18 of 20 oiled sites are saline marsh, against 5 of 25 unoiled sites. The
event study compares only within marsh type, which leaves effectively 18 against 5.

`python -m validation.oil_event_study` (vegetation cover primary, MODIS NDVI secondary):

| Outcome | Sites (oiled / unoiled) | Pre-spill check | Largest post-spill effect (90% interval) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vegetation cover | 20 / 24 | Passed narrowly (p = 0.106) | +7.9 points at 4 surveys (-2.5 to 18.4) |
| NDVI | 14 / 19 | Passed (p = 0.28) | +0.06 (-0.01 to 0.13) |

Reading: no detectable effect of oiling on vegetation at the CRMS stations. Given the distances above, this
says the monitoring network could not see the spill, not that the spill had no effect. One pre-spill
estimate (event time -2, the 2008 Gustav/Ike season) is +10.8 points with an interval excluding zero, so the
groups were not perfectly parallel before the spill.

Reading: the pre-specified analysis does not reproduce Mo, Kearney and Riter (2017). Its drop interval
includes zero, and fake spill dates in 2008-09 find shifts as large as the real one.

Exploratory only (looked at after the run, not pre-specified): the summer (June-September) oiled minus

oiled marsh, unoiled marsh and water; only 4 reference segments, 7 km away; Hurricane Isaac (2012) and
seasonal water levels add noise the reference may not share.

## 4. Treatment effects (Zengel et al. 2015)

`data/external/zengel2015/` holds the authors' own S1 Table (Excel) and a tidy copy (86 values).
`src/shoreline_remediation/treatment_effects.py` computes each treatment against no treatment, with a Welch
t interval (a two-group comparison that allows the groups to vary differently).

Total vegetation cover, percentage points versus no treatment (90% interval):

| Treatment | Year 1 (2011) | Year 2 (2012) | Year-2 cover as share of reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No treatment (baseline) | 0 | 0 | 0.19 |
| Manual | +15 (6 to 24) | +34 (19 to 49) | 0.55 |
| Mechanical | -6 (-10 to -2) | +5 (-12 to 22) | 0.24 |
| Planting after mechanical (randomized) | | Spartina +69 points (48 to 90); shoreline retreat -2.1 m/yr (-3.8 to -0.4) | |

Both treatments cut surface oil cover by about 40 points by year 2. Shoreline retreat under manual
treatment did not differ from no treatment.

Limits: one spill, one bay, heavily oiled Spartina marsh, 5 to 9 plots per class, two years. Treatments were
not randomly assigned (planting was), so these are observational contrasts. Mechanical treatment was applied
in May-June 2011, so its 2011 values show the marsh just after disturbance. No hold-out across spills is
possible; treat these as priors. `treatment_prior()` refuses to extrapolate beyond year 2 and flags other
habitats or oiling levels as out of scope.

## What this changes (written after sections 1-5; step 1 was then done, see section 6)

Both outcome datasets reachable today are too coarse for the narrow oiled marsh edge: CRMS plots are too far
from the edge, and Landsat pixels are too wide. The treatment contrasts in Zengel et al. are real
measurements, but from one site. Next steps:

1. NAIP aerial imagery (about 1 m, with a near-infrared band) resolves the oiled band itself. On Planetary
   Computer, northern Barataria Bay has NAIP for 2010, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2021 and 2023 (checked
   11 Nov 2026), but nothing before the spill, and the 2010 flight date relative to oiling is unknown. So it
   suits an oiled-versus-reference comparison of edge vegetation and shoreline retreat over time, not a
   before-after test. A pre-spill NAIP year (2007-2009) would have to come from USDA directly.
2. Choose more reference segments, closer to the oiled ones: widen the search box, or use segments with Very
   Light oiling as a dose comparison rather than a clean reference.
3. DIVER: look for segment-level treatment records (which segments were treated, how, when), to scale the
   treatment-effect priors beyond the Zengel plots.
4. Expert scores (needs Jaison).

## 6. Marsh-edge loss from NAIP aerial imagery (second analysis of the day)

Plan: `docs/NAIP_EDGE_LOSS_PLAN.md`, committed before any imagery was read; amendments committed after the
quality check and before the model was run. Scripts: `scripts/build_edge_cells.py`,
`scripts/naip_edge_extract.py`, `validation/naip_edge_loss.py` (checked first on simulated data, where it
recovers a known effect and finds none when none is planted).

Units: 100 m grid cells on SCAT-surveyed salt-marsh shoreline in Barataria Bay, each labelled with the worst
SCAT oiling recorded in it. Outcome: vegetated marsh lost from the cell between NAIP flights, expressed as
metres of edge retreat per year. Each oiled cell is compared with clean cells in the same 2 km block, adjusting
for how much open water surrounded the cell in 2010 (exposure to waves).

What changed after the quality check (all before the model ran): the land/water rule became a classifier
trained per tile on 2010 interior land and water; failed tiles and blocks are dropped; the hazy October 2013
flights could not be classified reliably, so the primary period moved from 2010-2013 to 2010-2015.

| Period | Cells (Heavy / clean) | 2 km blocks | Clean edge loss (m/yr) | Heavy minus clean, raw | Heavy minus clean, adjusted (90% interval) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2010-2015 (primary) | 4,779 (524 / 2,951) | 176 | 0.22 (-0.04 to 0.46) | 0.81 | **1.00 (0.58 to 1.43)** |
| 2010-2017 | 4,727 (516 / 2,989) | 180 | 0.25 (0.07 to 0.43) | 0.63 | 1.01 (0.65 to 1.38) |

Extra loss rises with oiling (2010-2015, m/yr vs clean): Very Light 0.31 (-0.23 to 0.85), Light 0.20 (-0.19 to
0.60), Moderate 0.69 (0.28 to 1.11), Heavy 1.00 (0.58 to 1.43). See `results/naip_edge_loss/`.

Benchmark: Zengel et al. (2015) measured an excess of about 1.2 to 2.3 m/yr on untreated heavily oiled plots
in 2011-12. The interval here overlaps the low end of that range. A smaller average is expected: these cells
cover all heavily oiled salt-marsh shoreline, most of it cleaned, over five to seven years, not the worst
untreated plots over two.

Reading: heavily oiled marsh edge lost roughly 1 m of extra edge per year compared with clean edge nearby,
for at least seven years, and the excess grows with oiling. [Withdrawn 12 Nov 2026: the duration claim is not supported; see section 11.] This is the first estimate in the project built
on thousands of real outcome units.

Cautions:
- Absolute rates are likely too low (clean edge 0.2 m/yr vs 0.8 to 1.2 m/yr on Zengel's reference plots),
  because one cell may hold more than 100 m of shoreline and 2 m pixels blur the edge. The difference
  between classes is more trustworthy than the absolute values.
- No pre-spill image: if heavily oiled shoreline was already eroding faster in the same 2 km block, part of the
  difference predates the spill. Exposure strongly predicts loss (3.5 m/yr per unit of open-water share), and
  oil landed on exposed shore, so residual exposure differences within blocks are the main threat.
- "Heavy" means heavy oiling plus whatever cleanup followed; Hurricane Isaac (2012) is inside both periods.
- One spill, so no hold-out across spills.

Next checks: a pre-spill erosion rate per cell (NAIP 2007 or 2009 from USDA, or Landsat 1999-2009) to test
whether oiled shore was already eroding faster; a finer exposure measure (fetch, or open water within 250 m);
segment-level treatment records from DIVER, to split Heavy by cleanup method, which would turn this into a
treatment-effect model at scale.

## 7. P1: pre-spill erosion check and finer exposure (proof-of-concept milestone P1)

Plan: `docs/P1_PRESPILL_CHECK_PLAN.md`, committed before any Landsat or new exposure value was computed. No
amendments were needed after the quality check. Scripts: `scripts/exposure_metrics.py`,
`scripts/landsat_edge_fraction.py`, `validation/p1_prespill_check.py` (tested first on simulated cells with a
known pre-spill gap and oil effect; it recovers both).

Measurement: Landsat short-wave infrared, unmixed per scene between that scene's own marsh and water values;
per-pixel median over 52 to 69 summer scenes per epoch (1998-2000 and 2007-2009 Landsat 5, 2014-2016 Landsat 8).
Quality check: cell values track NAIP (r = 0.68 in 2008/2010, 0.73 in 2015), reading about 0.1 lower because
30 m pixels mix land and water; the offset cancels in differences. Image: `results/p1_prespill/qa_landsat_epochs.png`.
New exposure measures from 2010 NAIP: open water within 250 m, and mean fetch (median 770 m).

Heavy minus clean edge loss, m/yr, same 2 km block, 90% interval (`results/p1_prespill/checks.csv`):

| Check (pre-specified) | Estimate | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 NAIP 2010-2015, as published | 1.00 (0.58 to 1.43) | Starting point |
| 1 Landsat 2008-2015 | 1.15 (0.78 to 1.53) | A second, independent sensor sees the same post-spill gap |
| 2 Landsat 1999-2008, before any oil | 0.30 (0.09 to 0.52) | Heavily oiled shore was already eroding faster |
| 3 Change after the spill minus change before | **0.85 (0.49 to 1.22)** | Pre-specified headline when check 2 fails |
| 4 NAIP 2010-2015 + water within 250 m + fetch | 0.47 (0.10 to 0.84) | Finer exposure explains about half of the 1.00 |

Pre-specified verdict: **Revised.** Part of the original gap predates the spill. Headline: 0.85 m/yr.

Exploratory (run once after the checks, not pre-specified; `results/p1_prespill/exploratory.csv`):

| Model | Estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Landsat 1999-2008 + finer exposure | 0.10 (-0.11 to 0.31): the pre-spill gap disappears |
| Landsat 2008-2015 + finer exposure | 0.72 (0.40 to 1.04) |
| After minus before + finer exposure | 0.62 (0.29 to 0.95) |

Reading: the pre-spill difference is explained by wave exposure. Heavily oiled shore faces open water, and
exposed marsh erodes faster with or without oil. After accounting for that, heavy oiling is associated with
about 0.6 to 0.85 m/yr of extra marsh-edge loss, and every version excludes zero. The original 1.00 m/yr
overstated the oil effect by roughly 15 to 40%.

Against Zengel et al. (2015), an excess of 1.2 to 2.3 m/yr on the worst untreated plots over two years: this
estimate is lower, as expected for an average over all heavily oiled salt-marsh shoreline, most of it cleaned,
over five to seven years.

Remaining limits: still one spill; exposure is measured, not modelled from waves; "Heavy" includes whatever
cleanup followed; Landsat 5 to Landsat 8 is a sensor change between the pre- and post-spill epochs (check 1
agrees with NAIP, which argues against a large sensor artefact).

Consequence for P2 (proposed D7): the predictive model should include the finer exposure measures (water within
250 m, fetch) and each cell's pre-spill trend, not only open water within 1 km.

## 8. P2: does the edge-loss model predict marsh it was not fitted to? (proof-of-concept milestone P2)

Plan: `docs/P2_BASIN_HOLDOUT_PLAN.md`, committed before any outcome outside Barataria Bay was measured;
amendments 1 to 4 committed before the model ran. Code: `validation/p2_basin_holdout.py` (tested on simulated
basins: a real oil effect transfers, pure noise does not beat the baseline). Results: `results/p2_basin_holdout/`.

Setup: 39,320 salt-marsh edge cells along the oiled Louisiana coast, split into basins by fixed geographic
lines. Each model is fitted on the other basins and predicts every cell of the held-out basin.
Predictors (all fixed by 2010): open water within 1 km and 250 m, fetch, pre-spill Landsat trend, and (M2)
SCAT oiling class. Outcome: NAIP edge loss 2010-2015.

Quality problems found before the run, and what was done (class-blind):
- The 2010 land/water rule fails where NAIP tiles are almost all water or carry bright river plume: water is
  labelled land. Areas failing a Landsat consistency check were excluded: the Mississippi birdfoot delta,
  eastern Breton/Chandeleur Sound, and south-west Barataria (lower bay, Caminada, Grand Isle). Cells where 2010
  NAIP and 2008 Landsat disagree by more than 0.4 were dropped (406 cells).
- These are applicability limits for the tool, and the land/water method itself is on the backlog, with
  Jaison's note that the P1 Landsat maps are not very clean.
- Terrebonne kept only 20 heavily oiled cells (threshold 30), so it was used for training only. Two basins
  were tested: Barataria (5,386 cells, 501 heavy) and western Breton (3,206 cells, 31 heavy).

Results (held-out basin; MAE = mean absolute error in m/yr; skill = error reduction against the no-predictor
baseline M0):

| Held-out basin | M0 baseline MAE | M1 exposure only | M2 exposure + oiling (primary) | M3 boosted trees | M2 90% interval coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barataria | 1.50 | 1.31 (skill 13%) | 1.34 (skill 10%) | 1.39 (7%) | 90% |
| Breton (west) | 1.42 | 1.37 (4%) | 1.36 (4%) | 1.45 (-2%) | 88% |
| Pooled | 1.47 | 1.33 (10%) | 1.35 (8%) | 1.41 (4%) | 89% |

Pre-specified verdict: **Partly transfers.** The model beats the no-information baseline in both tested
basins, and its 90% intervals hold (88 to 90% coverage), but adding the oiling class does not lower the
cell-level error compared with exposure alone. Boosted trees did worse than the linear model.

Oil effect transfer: fitted on Terrebonne + Breton, the Heavy uplift is 0.83 m/yr (0.37 to 1.30); Barataria's
own within-block estimate is 0.37 (0.03 to 0.70). Fitted on Terrebonne + Barataria it is 0.34 (0.18 to 0.50);
western Breton's own estimate is 0.76 (-0.65 to 2.17). The direction transfers; the size does not settle.

Exploratory, by oiling class in the held-out basins (`exploratory_by_class.csv`): heavily oiled Barataria
cells lost 1.07 m/yr against 0.33 for clean cells; the exposure-only model predicted 0.66 for them
(too low), the oil model 1.45 (too high). Interval coverage for heavy cells was 94% (Barataria) and 77%
(Breton, 31 cells).

Reading:
- What works: from exposure and pre-spill trend, the tool predicts edge loss on marsh it has not seen better
  than a blind guess, with honest 90% intervals. That is the PoC's generalisation evidence (within one spill).
- What does not yet: the oil-specific uplift is real on average (P1) but too small against cell-level noise
  (typical error about 1.4 m/yr) and too unstable between basins (0.3 to 0.8 m/yr) to sharpen predictions for
  individual 100 m cells. For planning, it should be shown as a range on top of the exposure-based prediction,
  not as a precise number.
- Measurement is now the main bottleneck: a more robust land/water classifier would cut the noise and return
  the excluded areas (backlog item).

Proposed D9 (for P5, the demo): predictions shown as the exposure-based estimate with its 90% interval, plus
an oiling uplift shown as a range (about 0.3 to 0.8 m/yr for heavy oiling); active river delta and open-water
plume areas flagged as out of scope.

Robustness of P1 to the P2 measurement problem (exploratory, `results/p1_prespill/robustness_agreement_filter.csv`):
re-running P1's headline after dropping the 312 Barataria cells (5 heavy) where 2010 NAIP and 2008 Landsat
disagree by more than 0.4 gives 0.80 m/yr (0.44 to 1.15), and 0.56 (0.24 to 0.87) with finer exposure. The P1
result is not driven by mis-classified tiles.

## 9. P3: cleanup treatment and marsh-edge erosion (proof-of-concept milestone P3)

Plan: `docs/P3_TREATMENT_PLAN.md`, committed before any treatment-group comparison. Code:
`validation/p3_treatment_erosion.py`, `tests/test_p3_treatment_erosion.py`; tool priors in
`treatment_effects.erosion_effects()` / `erosion_prior()`. Results: `results/p3_treatment/`.

**Data search.** No public, machine-readable record of which shoreline segments were cleaned, and how, was
found: SCAT layers carry no treatment fields; the DIVER marsh-cleanup study (14 Bay Jimmy sites, 2011) has no
treatment label per site; NOAA Technical Memorandum OR&R 42 shows the 11 km treated under STR S3-045 only as
map figures. A segment-level split of the NAIP cells is therefore not possible with open data. Found instead:
Zengel et al. (2022) plot data on GRIIDC (CC0): erosion by treatment group from aerial photos 1956-2018
(33 plots) and field measurements 2010-2016, with wave power; and Gibeaut's control-impact study in the DWH
administrative record (benchmark).

**A. Erosion change after the spill, net of each plot's 2005-2010 rate** (aerial photos; m/yr; 90% interval; values
corrected on 11 Nov 2026 to round from `results/p3_treatment/A_prespill_adjusted.csv` exactly, e.g. 1.8849 is 1.88, not 1.89):

| Contrast | 2010-2012 (primary) | 2012-2018 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oiled untreated vs unoiled reference | **+1.88 (1.57 to 2.20)** | +0.21 (-0.56 to 0.97) |
| Manual cleanup vs untreated | **-0.63 (-1.25 to -0.02)** | -0.13 (-0.62 to 0.36) |
| Mechanical cleanup vs untreated | +0.28 (-0.17 to 0.73) | -0.24 (-0.74 to 0.26) |
| Mechanical + planting vs untreated | **-1.47 (-2.06 to -0.88)** | -0.97 (-1.99 to 0.05) |

Plots: 5 reference, 9 untreated, 5 manual, 9 mechanical, 5 planted. With wave power added (A2), mechanical
becomes +0.61 (0.33 to 0.89) in 2010-2012; manual and planting are unchanged.

**B. Field-measured erosion, mean 2010-2016:** oiled untreated +0.77 (0.26 to 1.27) above reference; manual
-0.09 (-0.59 to 0.40) and mechanical -0.42 (-0.89 to 0.06) against untreated; no field data for planted plots.

**C. NAIP measurement against field data:** 17 plots fall in 7 NAIP edge cells. Spearman rank correlation
between the NAIP cell rate (2010-2015) and the plot's aerial-photo rate: 0.68 (90% bootstrap 0.30 to 0.91;
pre-specified criterion met: NAIP tracks field erosion). NAIP reads 1.2 m/yr lower on average. Caution: the
bootstrap resamples plots, but plots share cells, so the interval is optimistic.

Reading:
- Oiling roughly doubled to tripled marsh-edge erosion at the most heavily oiled sites for about two years,
  then erosion returned to near the pre-spill rate; the marsh lost meanwhile stays lost.
- Manual cleanup did not add erosion and may have reduced it slightly; mechanical cleanup was no better than
  leaving the marsh and possibly worse; planting after mechanical cleanup clearly reduced erosion. This matches
  Zengel et al.'s published conclusions and the direction of their 2015 vegetation results.
- Consistency check (exploratory arithmetic): two years at +1.88 then three at +0.21 averages about 0.9 m/yr
  over 2010-2015, close to P1's 0.85 m/yr for all heavily oiled shoreline, although P1 averages over cleaned
  and less severely oiled shoreline too. Gibeaut's treated-heavy vs untreated-less-oiled difference
  (0.41 m/yr, 2010-2013) is lower, as expected when the controls are also oiled.
- NAIP's ranking agrees with field data, but its absolute rates are about 1.2 m/yr too low: the demo should
  show NAIP-based predictions as relative (oiled vs clean) or add a calibration offset, and say so.

Limits: one site, 5 to 9 plots per group; treatments not randomly assigned except planting; mechanical plots
were the most heavily oiled; these plots overlap Zengel et al. (2015), so they are not independent evidence.

Option for Jaison (not done): digitise the treated stretches from the memorandum's maps (Figures 35, 36, 39)
to label NAIP cells treated/untreated. Imprecise, but it would give a first segment-scale check.

## 10. P5: the demo dashboard (proof-of-concept milestone P5)

Engineering, not a new analysis: plan `docs/P5_DEMO_PLAN.md` was committed before the build and fixed the model,
interval, flags and worked-example rule.

- **Deployed model (p5-edge-loss-v1):** P2's M1 (exposure + pre-spill trend, no oiling term) refitted on all
  12,696 valid cells in Terrebonne, Barataria and Breton. Held out by basin, its error is 1.30 m/yr against
  1.42 m/yr for predicting the average everywhere (about 9% better). The 90% interval is about -3.2 to +2.9 m/yr
  around each prediction: honest, and wide, because single 100 m cells are noisy. The artefact reproduces P2's
  held-out predictions exactly (test).
- **Worked example (rule fixed in advance):** the heavily oiled Barataria cell nearest Zengel's Bay Jimmy plots
  (69 m away). Expected 1.42 m/yr (90% interval -1.74 to 4.30); P2 held-out prediction 1.31; observed 1.54.
  Heavy-oiling uplift shown separately, 0.34 to 0.83 m/yr. Full write-up: `docs/P5_WORKED_EXAMPLE.md`.
- **Rules and evidence disagree on mechanical removal:** the FAST-like rules call it ineligible in salt marsh;
  Zengel 2022 found no clear extra erosion (+0.28 m/yr, -0.17 to 0.73) but those plots were the most heavily
  oiled. The demo shows both and lets neither override the other. Manual removal: rules eligible, evidence
  -0.63 m/yr erosion and +34 points vegetation cover at year 2 against natural recovery.
- **Found by the clean-install check:** `openpyxl` was missing from `requirements.txt` (needed to read the
  Zengel supplements); added. The dashboard otherwise runs without the analysis-only packages.

Interpretation for leads: the tool can say which sites are in scope, give an expected edge-loss rate with an
honest interval, and put field evidence beside each option. At single-cell scale the prediction is only slightly
better than the regional average; its value is in flagging scope and in the side-by-side evidence, not in
precise site forecasts.

| Oiling | SCAT maximum oiling class; Heavy vs No Oil Observed | "Heavily oiled"; classification not found; "heavy to very heavy (treated)" (secondary) | Binary: any SCAT oiling (light to heavy) vs none |
| Contrast | Heavy vs clean in the same 2 km block, net of the pre-spill difference | As worded in the abstract: one period against other periods within heavily oiled shoreline | Oiled vs unoiled islands, and against the pre-spill rate |
| Measurement | 30 m Landsat land fraction (2 m NAIP for check 0) | Field retreat measurements (secondary) | Island width and length in Google Earth imagery |
| Exposure | Open water, fetch, pre-spill trend | Raised as a confounder of other studies; own handling not found | Not found |
| Result | 0.85 m/yr (0.49 to 1.22) average over the interval | Significant extra loss only Fall 2010 to Fall 2011; 97-107% increase; 1 year for heavy, 3-4 years for very heavy treated (secondary); m/yr not found | Rate "increased to 275%" in first 6 months; 200% of unoiled over 2.5 years (abstract); no m/yr in abstract |

**Judgement.**
1. *The three do not contradict each other on whether heavy oiling raised erosion.* All find it did. They
   differ on how long, and P1 cannot speak to that: it is one average over a single post-spill interval.
2. *Duration.* Challenger: about 1 year (longer for very heavy treated shore, secondary). Zengel 2022 and our
   own P3: about 2 years (+1.88 m/yr in 2010-2012, then +0.21, -0.56 to 0.97). Turner: still double at 2.5
   years, when his record ends. So the literature range is roughly 1 to 2.5 years, and the papers disagree
   with one another, not only with us.
3. *Is 0.85 m/yr over 2010-2015 compatible with a short effect?* Yes, by arithmetic on our own P3 numbers
   (illustrative, not a new estimate): +1.88 m/yr for 2 years then +0.21 averages 0.91 m/yr over the 4.78-year
   NAIP interval and 0.68 over 7.13 years. Both fall inside the NAIP intervals (0.58 to 1.43; 0.65 to 1.38).
4. *But our data do not show the fade either.* The 2010-2015 and 2010-2017 NAIP estimates are almost equal
   (1.00 and 1.01), which implies about 1.04 m/yr of excess in 2015-2017 (arithmetic on the two published
   estimates; cell sets differ slightly; no interval). A fading oil effect predicts a falling average; a
   persistent difference in wave exposure predicts a constant one. Check 4 of P1 (finer exposure halves the
   estimate to 0.47) points the same way. Part of the P1 excess may therefore still be exposure. This is
   Challenger's central criticism of earlier studies, and it applies to ours.
5. *Estimands differ.* Challenger's abstract describes a period-against-period contrast within heavily oiled
   shore, Turner pools all oiling classes, P1 is Heavy against clean neighbours. The percentages (about 100%
   and 200%) cannot be turned into m/yr without the papers' rates.

**Verdict: note, not revision.** The 0.85 m/yr value stands as computed. Its wording must change:
- It is an *average over 2010-2015*, not an annual rate that can be projected forward. Published field
  evidence puts most of the excess in the first 1 to 2.5 years.
- Section 6's phrase "for at least seven years" is withdrawn: our data cannot distinguish seven years of
  about 1 m/yr from two years of about 2 m/yr followed by little (point 3), and three field studies favour
  the second.
- The demo's heavy-oiling uplift (D9, 0.3 to 0.8 m/yr) should carry the same note (proposed, not yet changed
  in the dashboard or the P6 report; needs Jaison's decision).

**What would settle it (proposed, not run; needs a plan committed first):** a period-split check on existing
cells: NAIP 2010-2015 against 2015-2017, and Landsat epochs around 2010-2012 and 2012-2015, with the P1
exposure adjustments. Prediction if the effect is oil: excess near zero after about 2013. If it stays near
1 m/yr, exposure confounding is the more likely reading and the headline should be cut towards 0.47.

**Could not be done:** full text of either paper; Challenger's source studies, oiling definition, statistics
and rates; Turner's group means (computable from his public file, not done here to avoid an unplanned
analysis). If Jaison can supply the two PDFs, the table above should be completed.

## 12. Period-split check: did the heavy-oiling excess fade? (12 Nov 2026)

Plan: `docs/PERIOD_SPLIT_CHECK_PLAN.md`, committed before any 2015-2017 rate was computed. Code:
`validation/period_split_check.py`, tested first on simulated cells (a planted fading effect is found; a
constant exposure difference shows no fade). Run once. Results: `results/period_split/`.

4,241 cells present in both NAIP periods, 159 blocks (516 Heavy, 2,542 clean). Model: P1 check 4 (block,
water within 1 km and 250 m, fetch) plus the pre-spill Landsat trend. Heavy minus clean, m/yr, 90% interval:

| Period | Estimate |
| --- | --- |
| 2010-2015 | 0.42 (0.07 to 0.76) |
| 2015-2017 | 0.28 (-0.27 to 0.84) |
| Difference (later minus earlier) | -0.14 (-0.80 to 0.52) |

Null baseline (classes where no oil effect on erosion is expected): Very Light shows nothing in either
period; **Light shows +0.78 (0.02 to 1.53) in 2015-2017**, against -0.18 in 2010-2015.

**Pre-specified verdict: inconclusive.** The later estimate is smaller but its interval is three times wider
(a 2.35-year window) and includes both zero and the earlier value.

Reading (orchestrator):
- The data cannot show a fade or rule one out. NAIP over two years is too noisy for this question.
- The Light result matters more than the Heavy one: an "effect" of 0.8 m/yr appears where none is expected.
  That is the size of unexplained differences this design can throw up in a two-year window, so a Heavy
  difference of 0.3 to 0.4 cannot be attributed to oil with confidence.
- With every adjustment the project has (finer exposure and pre-spill trend), the 2010-2015 excess is 0.42,
  not 0.85. The adjusted range across all pre-specified and exploratory models is now **0.42 to 0.85**, all
  excluding zero for 2010-2015.
- Part B of the plan (Landsat split at 2012) is **not run and not recommended**: Landsat 5 ended in November
  2011 and Landsat 8 began in 2013, so a 2011-2013 epoch would rest on Landsat 7 striped scenes and a
  sensor change inside the comparison. Field data (Zengel 2022: +1.88 then +0.21 m/yr) answer the
  duration question better than any image split we can make.
- Deviation from the plan: one added quality filter (block land share stable 2015-2017), which removed 14 cells.

Consequence: the headline stays "0.85 m/yr (0.49 to 1.22), average over 2010-2015" as pre-specified in P1,
but should always be quoted with the adjusted range 0.42 to 0.85 and the statement that its duration is
not resolved by our data. No dashboard number changes; the caption already says "average over 2010-2015".
