## 1. SCAT shoreline oiling (NCEI Accession 0163818)

Downloaded two Louisiana layers (raw files are not committed; `scripts/download_scat.sh` fetches them):

- 18611, maximum oiling per zone: 5,721 zones, with category (Very Light to Heavy) and the survey date of
  the maximum.
- 18612, every ground survey: 15,984 zone observations, including 7,077 "No Oil Observed" (NOO). The NOO
  records are what make a real unoiled comparison possible: shoreline that was surveyed and found clean.

## 2. CRMS sites against oiled shoreline, and the 2010 event study

`scripts/match_crms_scat.py` -> `data/external/scat_dwh/crms_scat_match.csv`

| Group (ASSUMED rule) | Sites |
| --- | --- |
| Oiled: Light or worse oiling within 1 km | 20 |
| Unoiled: surveyed shoreline within 1 km, no oil within 5 km | 25 |
| Excluded | 296 |

Distance check, done first as asked. Only 4 of the 20 oiled sites lie within 50 m of the surveyed marsh
edge (median 203 m), and only one (CRMS0176) lies within 50 m of an oiled zone. Deepwater Horizon oil sat in
roughly the first 10 to 20 m of marsh. So the CRMS stations mostly sit beyond the oiled band.

The groups are also unbalanced: 18 of 20 oiled sites are saline marsh, against 5 of 25 unoiled sites. The
event study compares only within marsh type, which leaves effectively 18 against 5.

`python -m validation.oil_event_study` (vegetation cover primary, MODIS NDVI secondary):

| Outcome | Sites (oiled / unoiled) | Pre-spill check | Largest post-spill effect (90% interval) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vegetation cover | 20 / 24 | Passed narrowly (p = 0.106) | +7.9 points at 4 surveys (-2.5 to 18.4) |
| NDVI | 14 / 19 | Passed (p = 0.28) | +0.06 (-0.01 to 0.13) |

Reading: no detectable effect of oiling on vegetation at the CRMS stations. Given the distances above, this
says the monitoring network could not see the spill, not that the spill had no effect. One pre-spill
estimate (event time -2, the 2008 Gustav/Ike season) is +10.8 points with an interval excluding zero, so the
groups were not perfectly parallel before the spill.

## 3. Landsat NDVI, northern Barataria Bay

`scripts/select_barataria_sites.py`, `scripts/landsat_ndvi.py`, `scripts/pool_ndvi_for_runner.py`.
Sites were chosen from SCAT alone before any NDVI was read:

- Oiled: 36 segments whose maximum oiling was Heavy, recorded in 2010 (34 had usable pixels).
- Reference: segments with only "No Oil Observed", more than 1 km from any oiled zone. Only 4 qualified
  in the box, a median 7 km from their oiled partners. This is the weakest part of the design.
- Pixels: 45 m either side of the shoreline line, kept only if vegetated before the spill (median
  May-October 2008-2009 NDVI at least 0.3, ASSUMED).
- Imagery: 1,044 Landsat 5, 7 and 8 Collection 2 Level-2 scenes, 2006-2016, from Microsoft Planetary
  Computer; clouds and shadows removed with the QA band. 26,529 scene-by-segment values.

`python -m validation.satellite_recovery data/external/landsat_barataria/ndvi_pooled.csv --spill-date 2010-06-01`
(spill date ASSUMED as heavy oil's arrival in northern Barataria):

| Run | Drop (90% interval) | Placebo check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary: pooled, plain model | +0.025 (-0.057 to 0.113) | Failed |
| Secondary: pooled, persistent model | +0.021 (-0.179 to 0.272) | Failed |
| Secondary: by reference segment (3 runnable) | 0.025 to 0.062; recovery times 14 years to the 115-year cap | Failed in all |

Reading: the pre-specified analysis does not reproduce Mo, Kearney and Riter (2017). Its drop interval
includes zero, and fake spill dates in 2008-09 find shifts as large as the real one.

Exploratory only (looked at after the run, not pre-specified): the summer (June-September) oiled minus
reference difference averaged about -0.04 in 2006-2010, -0.07 in 2011, -0.02 in 2012, and widened to
-0.08 to -0.10 in 2015-16. The 2011 dip matches the direction of Mo et al.; the later widening could be
edge erosion. Neither should be reported as a finding.

Likely reasons for the null: Landsat pixels (30 m) are wider than the oiled band, so each edge pixel mixes
oiled marsh, unoiled marsh and water; only 4 reference segments, 7 km away; Hurricane Isaac (2012) and
seasonal water levels add noise the reference may not share.

## 4. Treatment effects (Zengel et al. 2015)

`data/external/zengel2015/` holds the authors' own S1 Table (Excel) and a tidy copy (86 values).
`src/shoreline_remediation/treatment_effects.py` computes each treatment against no treatment, with a Welch
t interval (a two-group comparison that allows the groups to vary differently).

Total vegetation cover, percentage points versus no treatment (90% interval):

| Treatment | Year 1 (2011) | Year 2 (2012) | Year-2 cover as share of reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No treatment (baseline) | 0 | 0 | 0.19 |
| Manual | +15 (6 to 24) | +34 (19 to 49) | 0.55 |
| Mechanical | -6 (-10 to -2) | +5 (-12 to 22) | 0.24 |
| Planting after mechanical (randomized) | | Spartina +69 points (48 to 90); shoreline retreat -2.1 m/yr (-3.8 to -0.4) | |

Both treatments cut surface oil cover by about 40 points by year 2. Shoreline retreat under manual
treatment did not differ from no treatment.

Limits: one spill, one bay, heavily oiled Spartina marsh, 5 to 9 plots per class, two years. Treatments were
not randomly assigned (planting was), so these are observational contrasts. Mechanical treatment was applied
in May-June 2011, so its 2011 values show the marsh just after disturbance. No hold-out across spills is
possible; treat these as priors. `treatment_prior()` refuses to extrapolate beyond year 2 and flags other
habitats or oiling levels as out of scope.

## What this changes

Both outcome datasets reachable today are too coarse for the narrow oiled marsh edge: CRMS plots are too far
from the edge, and Landsat pixels are too wide. The treatment contrasts in Zengel et al. are real
measurements, but from one site. Next steps:

1. NAIP aerial imagery (about 1 m, with a near-infrared band) resolves the oiled band itself. On Planetary
   Computer, northern Barataria Bay has NAIP for 2010, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2021 and 2023 (checked
   11 Nov 2026), but nothing before the spill, and the 2010 flight date relative to oiling is unknown. So it
   suits an oiled-versus-reference comparison of edge vegetation and shoreline retreat over time, not a
   before-after test. A pre-spill NAIP year (2007-2009) would have to come from USDA directly.
2. Choose more reference segments, closer to the oiled ones: widen the search box, or use segments with Very
   Light oiling as a dose comparison rather than a clean reference.
3. DIVER: look for segment-level treatment records (which segments were treated, how, when), to scale the
   treatment-effect priors beyond the Zengel plots.
4. Expert scores (needs Jaison).
