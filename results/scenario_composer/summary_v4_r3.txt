Overall R3 reading (added after all runs; nothing above was rerun or retuned after seeing these numbers).

The diagnostic confirmed the double count (Heavy in-sample residual +0.434 m/yr, 90% CI +0.224 to +0.697, entirely
above zero), so the clean-edge refit (primary fix, D27) was expected to and did remove that specific mechanism:
a model that never saw Heavy, Moderate or Light cells during training can no longer be absorbing part of their
extra erosion before the Zengel excess is added on top.

Coverage (the pre-specified, unchanged 80-97% gate) PASSES in every b-check (C1b 93.2%, C2b 89.9%, B1b cells
92.3%/blocks 92.2%), same as the originals, and the NOO-bias-not-worse-than-original gate PASSES everywhere too
(differences of -0.013, +0.011 and +0.047 m/yr, all well inside the 0.10 m/yr allowance) -- the fix did not make
clean-cell predictions worse.

The NEW heavy-bias gate (+/-0.25 m/yr) DOES NOT PASS in any of the three checks: heavy bias is +0.549 m/yr (C1b,
was +0.57 m/yr under the original v1 baseline), +0.608 m/yr (C2b, no original comparator computed by the plan),
and +0.589 m/yr (B1b, was +0.534 m/yr originally -- slightly WORSE, not better). So the fix is correctly built
and removes the identified in-sample mechanism, but it does not, on its own, bring the heavy-cell bias within the
new gate, and in one check (B1b) the bias moved in the wrong direction.

What weakens the conclusion / plain reading: the clean-edge model was fitted on about 85% of the original cells
but with far fewer blocks near Heavy-oiled shoreline, so its coefficients differ non-trivially from v1's (e.g.
pre_rate 0.222 -> 0.249, log_fetch 0.628 -> 0.593) and shift predictions for every cell, not just Heavy ones, in
a direction that is not guaranteed to reduce Heavy bias specifically -- it happened to help a little in C1b
(barataria and breton both improved slightly) and to hurt a little in B1b (barataria alone, different held-out
pool). A large share of the measured bias comes from sources the fix does not touch and that were already named
against the originals: NAIP reads about 1.2 m/yr low against field erosion plots, most heavily oiled shoreline
was actually cleaned (so real-world erosion is lower than the untreated-plot Zengel excess used here), and the
single pooled leave-one-basin-out residual (mixing all oiling classes, as in the originals) carries its own
basin-transfer bias -- breton in particular shows a large positive NOO-cell prediction gap (~+0.58 m/yr, checked
directly, not from a model defect but from basin-to-basin transfer) that inflates both the clean and the original
bias figures by similar amounts, which is why the clean-vs-original comparison stays small even though the
absolute bias is large in both. In short: the double count was real and is now fixed, but it was not the main
source of the heavy-cell over-prediction documented in B1/C1; the larger remaining sources (measurement gap,
cleaned-vs-untreated mismatch, basin transfer) are outside this fix's scope and remain open, as the original
checks already said.


Orchestrator reading (added after the run, 14 Nov 2026; no rerun). The pre-specified confirmation rule was mis-specified.
A positive Heavy in-sample residual (+0.434) means M1 UNDER-predicts heavy cells, i.e. M1 did not absorb the oil effect;
a double count would show a residual near zero. Absorption is the shift in heavy-cell predictions between the all-cell and
clean-cell fits, which is small (heavy bias changes -0.02 to +0.06 m/yr across C1b, C2b, B1b). So the double count is minor.
The over-prediction comes from the size of the added excess: the Zengel untreated-plot excess averages about 0.88 m/yr over
five years (2 x 1.88 + 3 x 0.21) / 5, while NAIP shows about 0.43 m/yr beyond exposure and pre-spill trend (this diagnostic).
Candidate correction (new decision D32, needs its own plan): scale or replace the oil excess with a NAIP-based within-model
estimate, or keep Zengel and show the bias. Relative to NOO cells in the same held-out basins, the C1b heavy excess bias is
+0.12 m/yr (0.549 - 0.428); in B1b it is +0.48 (0.589 - 0.111), so part of the C1 gap is a basin-level shift common to all cells.
