# Treatment evidence notes (salt marsh primary, mangrove secondary)

Scope and access caveats. The NOAA marsh guidance I read is the March 2022 update (Michel, Rutherford, Zengel), not the 2013 edition. I read the first ~200,000 of 338,143 characters (Chapters 2-3, partial). The NOAA 2014 mangrove guidance was read only to character 100,000 (method sections cut off). All "FULL TEXT" items were read through a WebFetch extraction, not a native PDF read, so numbers should be re-checked against the originals before being used as prior ranges. Primary studies cited inside guidance or reviews are SECONDARY. Almost all primary papers were not opened. Habitat note: Delaware Bay bioremediation trials were on sand beaches, not marsh.

## Nutrient addition / biostimulation
- Supports: fertilizer raised Spartina growth and, in greenhouse sods, oil degradation (81% lower targeted normal hydrocarbons, 17% lower aromatics vs oiled control; Lin et al 1999). The MMS 2002 summary says the oil loss occurred only when plants were present.
- Strength: weak for field use. About 3 independent greenhouse reports from one research group (LSU), no marsh field trial opened. NOAA 2022 says benefits are limited to surface oil, aromatics not clearly faster, and oxygen is often limiting.
- Harms or limits: nutrient export (P more than N) and sensitive plant communities (Pezeshki 2000 review).
- Not verified: Lee et al, Tate et al 2012, Venosa field trials; actual field marsh numbers.

## Bioaugmentation (microbial products)
- Supports: no benefit. Two greenhouse reports (same project) found no effect on degradation; Pezeshki 2000 says no clear gain over nutrients.
- Strength: weak, greenhouse only, one product, one lab group. No field trial in marsh or mangrove found (row recorded as none).
- Not verified: other products, cold or low-microbe sites.

## In-situ burning
- Supports: removes more than 95% of floating oil (mesocosm). Field recovery counts: 24 spills, 46% recovered under 1.5 years, 12% over 10 years (NOAA 2022 synthesis, not a controlled comparison). Baustian 2010 reports good recovery after a Louisiana burn.
- Strength: moderate. Several spills plus 3 field experiments (as reported by NOAA), but mostly secondary; the Louisiana group dominates.
- Conditions: helps with water cover (10 cm kept soil under 37 C); harms when soil is dry or drained (soil about 100-120 C, little regrowth), at high oil dose (Smith and Proffitt 1999), and with burn residues. Does not remove oil already in soil. Conflict: Copano Bay TX burn had worse cover and diversity than unoiled control after 2 years (Hoff 1995).
- Not verified: Baustian 2010 numbers; Lin 2005; Mendelssohn 1995 season percentages (secondary).

## Vegetation cutting
- Supports: mostly no benefit or harm for recovery. NOAA Table 3-2: 29 cases, 34% positive, 28% no difference, 38% negative; in 19 cut-vs-uncut comparisons 21% positive. Brazil field trial found cut and uncut both recovered in 6 months (Wolinski 2011). Louisiana plot: cutting slowest (2.5 yr vs 3 months, Hoff 1995).
- Strength: moderate. Many mixed cases, but aggregated via guidance. Positive cases mostly fall/winter cuts or when needed for access to thick oil or Phragmites.
- Not verified: the table values (the extraction had an inconsistency), primary studies. No mangrove data found.

## Low-pressure ambient flushing
- Supports: useful for fresh liquid oil with 50 psi or lower pressure on a falling tide; ineffective on thick emulsified oil and risks erosion. Louisiana experimental plots flushed recovered in 3 months, like no cleanup.
- Strength: weak to moderate; case studies often combined with trampling and vacuuming (Fidalgo Bay 3-4 years).
- Not verified: quantified oil removal; primary studies.

## Passive sorbents
- Supports: no effect on recovery that can be isolated. Julie N marsh recovered in one season with only passive sorbents, but there was no untreated control; Panama mangrove plot used sorbents on floating oil with unrecovered forest at 32 years.
- Strength: very weak (case examples, guidance).
- Harms: waste volume, stranded boom persisting about 3 years after Deepwater Horizon.

## Vacuum / skimming of pooled oil
- Supports: removes large quantities of pooled oil early; best on liquid or heavy oil. Hard nozzles gouged marsh at Deepwater Horizon.
- Strength: weak (guidance and 2 spill cases, no controls).
- Not verified: any quantified comparison.

## Surface washing agents / shoreline cleaners
- Supports: greenhouse and field tests show rescue of Bunker C-oiled Spartina and faster crude recovery when applied 1-2 days after oiling (Pezeshki series); full-scale Julie N test (Corexit 9580, 9 days after oiling) removed about 50% of oil on one leaf side; long-term crude outcome similar to oil alone; Bizzell 1999 and Deepwater Horizon found little effect. Mangrove survival benefit only within 7 days (Teas 1993, secondary).
- Strength: weak to moderate; conflicting. Timing is decisive.
- Not verified: primary papers; the Pezeshki 2005 abstract does not name the product.

## Sediment removal / excavation
- Supports: harmful when aggressive. Amoco Cadiz Ile Grande: 5 to over 8 years; Miguasha: more than 11 years; Challenger 2008 BC: near-complete mortality. Success only when elevation restored plus replanting (Chalk Point).
- Strength: moderate in direction (3 independent cases, all spill monitoring, no controlled trial).
- Not verified: primary papers and sample sizes.

## Replanting after cleanup
- Supports in marsh: beneficial (Arthur Kill, Chalk Point) per guidance; Zengel DWH results excluded. Mangrove: negative or no benefit. Duke 1996 found planted plots less dense and shorter than natural recruitment; TROPICS juveniles planted in oiled sediment died within 4 months.
- Strength: weak outside DWH.
- Not verified: Bergen et al 2000 primary.

## Trampling / foot traffic
- Supports: harmful. Challenger 2008, Hoff 1993 (more mortality than oil), Mendelssohn 1990 (long-lasting; later attributed to lower elevation, not oil).
- Strength: moderate in direction, but effect confounded with removal; no controlled trampling experiment opened. Walking boards advised.

## Natural attenuation (reference)
- Supports: recovery in 1-2 growing seasons for light to moderate oiling (32 spills/experiments, Michel and Rutherford 2014 abstract); fastest in warm climates and with less intervention; slowest with thick oil, sheltered sites, cold climates. Counter-cases: Wales spill 15 years; Metula Chile 20+ years; mangrove (Panama) not recovered at 32 years for heavy untreated crude.
- Strength: strongest of all treatments (many independent spills and plots), though selection bias is likely.
- Not verified: full text of Michel and Rutherford 2014 (abstract only).
